\begin{abstract}
We present line-of-sight magnetic field ($B_\parallel$) measurements across
the Perseus Molecular Cloud (PMC), derived from Faraday rotation measures in
a dedicated Very Large Array L-band polarization survey. Applying the MC-BLOS
(Molecular Cloud Line-of-Sight magnetic field) technique to 197 background
polarized sources, we obtain the most densely sampled $B_\parallel$ map of
the cloud to date, with a mean nearest-neighbour separation of
$\approx 1.4$~pc, a factor of $\sim 5$ improvement in source density
over previous rotation measure catalogs of this region. We determine a
reference rotation measure of $31.6 \pm 6.1$~rad~m$^{-2}$ (standard error;
sample scatter 17.2~rad~m$^{-2}$), consistent with earlier estimates. The
derived fields span $-991$ to $+858$~$\mu$G with a median absolute strength
of 116~$\mu$G. The inferred absolute strengths are model-dependent because
the electron column is obtained from the extinction-based chemical model. The
distribution of field directions is not random: 122 of 197 sight lines (62\%) are directed toward the observer and
are concentrated in the north and west of the cloud, while fields directed
away dominate the south and east. A two-sided exact binomial test rejects an
equal split at $p=9.98\times10^{-4}$. The sign split is sensitive to the
reference rotation measure, so the adopted zero point is set by the stability
analysis. This sign pattern is consistent with the specific
prediction of the shock-cloud-interaction formation model as applied to
Perseus by \citet{tahani2022b}, which requires a concave arc-shaped field
with the reversal oriented in exactly this sense, and places on a firm
observational footing a reversal previously constrained by only $\sim 10$
sight lines \citep{tahani2018}. The apparent anticorrelation between
$|B_\parallel|$ and visual extinction is partly induced by the dependence of
the modeled electron column on extinction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Magnetic fields are fundamental to the physics of molecular clouds,
influencing their formation, structure, and evolution toward star formation
\citep{mckee2007,crutcher2012magnetic,hennebelle2019role,pattle2023,liu2022magnetic}.
In the low-density envelopes of molecular clouds, magnetic pressure can
provide significant support against gravitational collapse
\citep{mouschovias1976nonhomologous}, while in denser regions the field
regulates angular momentum transport during core collapse through magnetic
braking \citep{pudritz2019}. The interplay between magnetic fields,
turbulence, and gravity determines the star formation efficiency and the
initial mass function of newly formed stars
\citep{krumholz2019,federrath2015inefficient}, and shapes the filamentary
structures in which star formation proceeds
\citep{hartmann2001rapid,li2014link,suin2025role}.

Understanding the role of magnetic fields in molecular clouds requires
observational constraints on both the field strength and its
three-dimensional (3D) orientation \citep{tahani2022three}. The plane-of-sky
component, $B_\perp$, can be traced through polarized thermal emission from
magnetically aligned dust grains
\citep{andersson2015interstellar,planck2016,ade2016planck,soler2019using},
now routinely resolved on sub-parsec scales in individual clouds
\citep{tahani2023jcmt,hwang2025jcmt}, or through polarized starlight
absorption \citep{heiles20009286}. Interpreting dust polarization in terms of
field geometry is not always straightforward, however, particularly at high
column density \citep{valdivia2022mm,le2020statistical}. The line-of-sight component, $B_\parallel$, is more difficult to measure.
Zeeman splitting provides a direct measurement but is limited to specific
molecular transitions and requires long integration times even for the
strongest fields
\citep{crutcher1999detection,crutcher2010magnetic,crutcher2019review}.

Faraday rotation is the rotation of the plane of polarization of an
electromagnetic wave as it propagates through a magnetized, ionized medium.
The medium is birefringent to left- and right-hand circularly polarized
radiation, so the two components accumulate a phase difference that rotates
the polarization angle of the emergent wave by an amount proportional to
$\lambda^2$ \citep{burn1966,brentjens2005}. Because it depends on both the
free electron density and the magnetic field component along the path,
Faraday rotation of background polarized radio sources can probe
$B_\parallel$ across an entire cloud complex
\citep{wolleben2004,tahani2018,takahashi2021introduction}.

The observed polarization angle varies with wavelength as
$\Psi(\lambda) = \Psi_0 + \Phi\lambda^2$, where $\Psi_0$ is the intrinsic
polarization angle, $\lambda$ is the wavelength in meters, and $\Phi$ is the
Faraday depth,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rm-basic}
    \Phi = 0.812 \int_{\rm source}^{\rm observer} n_e\,B_\parallel \, dl
    \;\;[\mathrm{rad\,m^{-2}}],
\end{equation}
with $n_e$ the free electron density in cm$^{-3}$, $B_\parallel$ the
line-of-sight magnetic field component in $\mu$G (positive toward the
observer), and $dl$ the path length in pc \citep{han2017observing}. The
observed rotation measure, $\mathrm{RM} = d\Psi/d\lambda^2$, is the net
Faraday depth along the full path to the source and equals $\Phi$ only for a
single Faraday-rotating screen.

For a sight line passing through a molecular cloud, the observed RM includes
contributions from the Galactic foreground, the cloud itself, and the diffuse
background:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rm-split}
    \mathrm{RM_{obs}} = \mathrm{RM_{fg}} + \mathrm{RM_{cloud}} + \mathrm{RM_{bg}}.
\end{equation}
Isolating the cloud contribution requires subtraction of the foreground and
background components. The Galactic RM sky is structured on a wide range of
angular scales \citep{simonetti1984small,haverkorn2008outer,stil2010structure},
and shows a systematic dependence on Galactic latitude
\citep{schnitzeler2010latitude,van2011modeling}, so this subtraction is not a
trivial constant offset. It can be estimated from reference sight lines that
do not intersect the cloud \citep{tahani2018,tahani2025mcblos} or, in
principle, from all-sky reconstructions of the Galactic Faraday rotation sky
\citep{hutschenreuter2022galactic}, which are increasingly well constrained
by wide-area RM grids \citep{o2023faraday}.

The Perseus Molecular Cloud (PMC) is a well-suited target for Faraday
rotation studies of $B_\parallel$. Located at a distance of $294 \pm 17$~pc
\citep{zucker2018}, it is one of the nearest active star-forming regions
\citep{enoch2006bolocam,hatchell2007star,tobin2016vla}, with
well-characterized dust column density maps from \textit{Herschel}
\citep{andre2010}, near-infrared extinction maps
\citep{kainulainen2009,lombardi2009nicest}, and extensive molecular line and
kinematic observations
\citep{ridge2006complete,curtis2010submillimetre,chen2024filament,scibelli2024survey}.
Its high Galactic latitude ($b \simeq -20^\circ$) places it toward the
anticenter, reducing line-of-sight confusion relative to clouds in the disk
\citep{tahani2022b}.

Previous $B_\parallel$ studies of Perseus used the NVSS-based RM catalog of
\citet{taylor2009}, derived from the survey of \citet{condon1998nrao}, and
found the line-of-sight field to reverse direction across the cloud's minor
axis \citep{tahani2018}. This reversal, combined with \textit{Planck}
$B_\perp$ observations \citep{planck2016} and Galactic magnetic field models
\citep{jansson2012new}, was used to reconstruct a concave arc-shaped 3D field
morphology for the cloud \citep{tahani2022b}, following the approach
previously developed for Orion~A
\citep{tahani2019could,tahani2022orion} and reviewed by
\citet{tahani2022three}.

The arc-shaped morphology is a specific prediction of the shock-cloud
interaction (SCI) picture of filamentary cloud formation, in which a shock
front sweeps a pre-existing inhomogeneous cloud and bends the component of
the ambient field perpendicular to the propagation direction
\citep{inutsuka2015formation,inoue2018formation}; an analogous geometry was proposed
for Orion~A by \citet{heiles1997}. For Perseus, the relevant compressions are
associated with the Per-Tau shell \citep{bialy2021,doi2021two} and, as argued
by \citet{tahani2022b}, a second structure behind the cloud, a picture that
received independent support from kinematic observations
\citep{kounkel2022}. Given the modeled Galactic field direction and the sign
of the CO velocity in the co-moving \ion{H}{1} frame, the SCI model predicts
a concave arc and therefore a specific sense for the reversal: fields
directed away from the observer on the southeast side of the cloud and toward
the observer on the northwest side. Testing that directional prediction
requires dense $B_\parallel$ sampling across the reversal.

Earlier work was limited by the low areal source density of NVSS-based RM
catalogs ($\sim 3$~deg$^{-2}$), which provided only $\sim 10$ usable sight
lines through the cloud. Such sparse sampling precluded detailed mapping of
the $B_\parallel$ structure and left the sharpness, location, and spatial
extent of the reversal essentially unconstrained. Foreground complexity is a
further concern: \citet{doi2021two} showed that optical stellar polarimetry
toward Perseus separates into two components, one tracing the cloud at
$\sim 300$~pc and one tracing an unrelated foreground cloud at $\sim 150$~pc,
so a small reference sample risks conflating the two.

In Paper~I \citep{hajizadeh2026comprehensive} we presented a dedicated VLA
L-band polarization survey of the Perseus Molecular Cloud that substantially improves upon
previous RM coverage. The survey covers $\sim 13.8$~deg$^2$ with an areal
source density of $\sim 15$~deg$^{-2}$, providing 206 high-confidence
polarized sources with wideband RM measurements. This catalog enables the
first densely sampled mapping of $B_\parallel$ across the entire Perseus Molecular Cloud.

In this paper we apply the MC-BLOS (Molecular Cloud Line-Of-Sight magnetic
field) technique \citep{tahani2018,tahani2025mcblos} to our VLA RM catalog to
derive $B_\parallel$ at 197 positions across the cloud. We present the
resulting map, test the statistical significance and robustness of the
spatial pattern of field directions, compare that pattern with the SCI
prediction, and discuss the implications for the magnetic field structure of
the Perseus molecular cloud.

The paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:data} summarizes the
observational data. Section~\ref{sec:method} describes the MC-BLOS
methodology. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the $B_\parallel$
measurements and their spatial properties. Section~\ref{sec:discussion}
discusses the implications and compares with previous results.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} summarizes our conclusions.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Rotation Measure Catalog}

The RM catalog used here is drawn from the wideband VLA L-band polarization
survey presented in Paper~I \citep{hajizadeh2026comprehensive}. The survey
covers $\sim 13.8$~deg$^2$ toward the Perseus molecular cloud at declinations
$28^\circ < Dec < 33^\circ$ and right ascensions
$49^\circ < RA < 56^\circ$. Observations span
930--2070~MHz across nine relatively RFI-free spectral windows, providing a
fractional bandwidth $\Delta\lambda^2/\lambda^2 \sim 0.8$ that is essential
for unambiguous RM determination \citep{brentjens2005}.

The mean Stokes~$I$ sensitivity is $\sim 80$~$\mu$Jy~beam$^{-1}$, yielding a
catalog of 1410 compact radio sources. RMs were derived using RM Synthesis
\citep{burn1966,brentjens2005} and RM CLEAN \citep{heald2009}, with a
conservative $8\sigma$ threshold in polarized intensity. The final RM catalog
contains 205 high-confidence polarized sources, corresponding to 
source density of $\sim 15$~deg$^{-2}$, a factor of $\sim 5$ higher than the
NVSS-based catalog of \citet{taylor2009}. At the distance of the Perseus molecular cloud, this
corresponds to a mean nearest-neighbor separation of $\approx 1.4$~pc, which
sets the finest spatial scale on which the present analysis can resolve
structure in $B_\parallel$.

\subsection{Extinction Map}

The MC-BLOS technique requires an independent tracer of cloud column density
in order to (a) classify sight lines as ON-cloud or OFF-cloud according to
extinction thresholds, and (b) compute the electron column density along each
sight line via chemical modeling. We adopt the visual extinction ($A_V$) map
of the PMC derived from \textit{Herschel} Gould Belt Survey SPIRE/PACS
observations \citep{andre2010}, consistent with the default cloud maps
distributed with MC-BLOS \citep{tahani2025mcblos}. Following
\citet{tahani2025mcblos}, extinction is related to hydrogen column density
through the empirical factor
$N_{\rm H}/A_V = 2.21 \times 10^{21}$~cm$^{-2}$~mag$^{-1}$
\citep{guver2009relation}. This map covers the full spatial extent of our RM
catalog footprint at resolution sufficient for the MC-BLOS analysis.

\section{Methodology}
  \label{sec:method}

  \subsection{The MC-BLOS Software}

  The analysis presented here was carried out with \texttt{MC-BLOS} (v1.0), the
  publicly available Python
  package\footnote{\url{https://github.com/MehrnooshTahani/MC-BLOS}} that
  implements the Faraday-rotation technique of \citet{tahani2018} in automated
  form \citep{tahani2025mcblos}. Our contribution is
  the input RM catalog from Paper~I, the choice of input parameters for Perseus,
  and the analysis of the resulting $B_\parallel$ measurements. We summarize
  below only what is needed to interpret our results, and refer the reader to
  \citet{tahani2018} and \citet{tahani2025mcblos} for the full description of
  the technique and the software.

  The software takes three inputs: a catalog of Faraday rotation measures for
  background point sources, a map of extinction or column density for the cloud,
  and output from a chemical evolution code. From these it identifies reference
  sight lines, isolates the RM induced by the cloud, and converts that RM into
  $B_\parallel$ at each remaining source position, together with uncertainties.

  \subsection{Reference Point Selection}

  MC-BLOS separates the cloud's contribution to the observed RM from the
  Galactic foreground and background using an ON--OFF approach
  \citep{tahani2018}. Sources with extinction below a threshold $A_{V,\rm th}$
  are treated as potential OFF points; the threshold is set by the cloud's
  Galactic location. Perseus lies off the Galactic disk at $b \simeq -20^\circ$,
  so the software adopts its high-latitude default of
  $A_{V,\rm th} = 1.0$~mag.

  The software then rejects potential OFF points that lie too close to
  high-extinction regions, where the cloud's own field could contribute, and
  those whose RM is a statistical outlier relative to the rest of the OFF
  sample. The proximity and outlier criteria are controlled by the multipliers listed
in Table~\ref{tab:params}. For Perseus, these reject sources within
30~arcmin (2.6~pc at the cloud distance) of regions with $A_V \geq 5$~mag,
and RM values more than five interquartile ranges from the OFF-sample median.

  The number of reference points used is set by the stability trend
  analysis of \citet{tahani2018}, automated in v1.0. $B_\parallel$ is computed
  for every ON sight line as OFF points are added in order of increasing
  extinction, and the number at which the resulting values stabilize is adopted.
  We present the outcome of this analysis for our catalog in
  Section~\ref{sec:rmref}.

  \subsection{Cloud RM and Field Direction}

  For each ON point the cloud's contribution is the difference between the
  observed RM and the reference value,
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:rmcloud}
      \mathrm{RM_{cloud}} = \mathrm{RM_{obs}} - \mathrm{RM_{ref}},
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathrm{RM_{ref}}$ is the average RM of the selected reference points.
  A positive $\mathrm{RM_{cloud}}$ corresponds to a field directed toward the
  observer and a negative value to a field directed away. Because the same
  scalar $\mathrm{RM_{ref}}$ is subtracted from every point, the resulting
  sign pattern depends on this single zero point, and we test its robustness in
  Section~\ref{sec:rmref-sensitivity}.

  \subsection{Electron Column Density and Field Strength}
  \label{sec:ne}

  Field strength requires the electron column density along each line of sight.
  Rather than assuming a fixed ionization fraction, MC-BLOS reads the electron
  abundance from a chemical evolution code \citep{gibson2009} using the UMIST
  Rate99 reaction network \citep{leteuff2000}. The cloud is treated as a
  symmetric plane-parallel slab: the abundance is summed layer by layer from the
  edge inward until half the total extinction is reached, and the result is
  doubled to account for the far side \citep[see Figure~1
  of][]{tahani2025mcblos}. This captures the change in ionization conditions
  between diffuse envelopes and dense interiors, which a single ionization
  fraction would not.

  Writing the electron column as $N_e = \int n_e \, dl$ in
  cm$^{-3}$~pc, consistent with Equation~\ref{eq:rm-basic}, the field strength
  follows from
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:blos}
      B_\parallel \;[\mu\mathrm{G}] =
      \frac{\mathrm{RM_{cloud}} \;[\mathrm{rad\,m^{-2}}]}
           {0.812 \, N_e \;[\mathrm{cm^{-3}\,pc}]}.
  \end{equation}

  \subsection{Input Parameters}

  Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the parameters we adopted for Perseus. We ran the
  software with its default configuration except where noted, having first
  inspected the diagnostic outputs as recommended by \citet{tahani2025mcblos}. The chemical

  \subsection{Uncertainties}

  MC-BLOS propagates four contributions to $\delta B_\parallel$, combined in
  quadrature \citep[Equation~5 of][]{tahani2025mcblos}: the tabulated
  uncertainty on $\mathrm{RM_{obs}}$; the standard error of $\mathrm{RM_{ref}}$
  across the selected reference points; the extinction uncertainty, taken from
  the range of $A_V$ within a Jeans length of each source position; and the
  sensitivity of the chemical model, evaluated by varying the input temperature
  by $\pm 20\%$ ($T_0 = 9.6$--$14.4$~K) and the volume density by $\pm 50\%$
  ($n_0 = 500$--$1500$~cm$^{-3}$).

  The RM terms can reverse the sign of $B_\parallel$, whereas the extinction and
  chemical model terms affect only its magnitude \citep{tahani2025mcblos}. We report the total uncertainty for each point, and separately assess in
Section~\ref{sec:signs} how many field directions are significant against the
RM-related terms alone.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Reference RM Determination}
\label{sec:rmref}

Of the 206 polarized sources in the Paper~I catalog, 205 fall within the
\textit{Herschel} extinction map footprint. After applying the extinction threshold and
proximity filters, 14 candidate reference points remain.
Figure~\ref{fig:stability} shows the stability-trend analysis: the median
$B_\parallel$ converges rapidly and the percentile spread stabilizes by
$N = 8$ reference points, which we adopt.

\end{equation}
where the quoted uncertainty is the standard error of the mean; the
sample standard deviation is 17.2~rad~m$^{-2}$.

of the reference sample.

\subsection{Spatial Distribution of Field Directions}
\label{sec:signs}

Of the 197 measurements, 122 (62\%) have positive $B_\parallel$ and 75 (38\%)
have negative $B_\parallel$. Using the RM measurement and reference-sample
scatter terms alone, 96 positive and 56 negative measurements have a sign
significant at $>2\sigma$; the remaining measurements are formally
consistent with zero under this criterion.

The division between positive and negative fields is not spatially random.
Positive fields are concentrated in the northern and western portions of the
cloud, while negative fields dominate the southern and eastern regions
(Figure~\ref{fig:blos-map}). We quantify this in the following two
subsections.

The transition between the two regions does not follow a simple boundary.
There are localized regions of mixed polarity, particularly along the main
filamentary ridge, which may reflect small-scale magnetic structure,
variations in line-of-sight geometry, or the sight lines whose signs are not
individually significant.

\subsubsection{Statistical Significance of the Sign Asymmetry}
\label{sec:sign-tests}

A two-sided exact binomial test of 122 positive detections out of 197 against
an equal-probability null hypothesis gives $p=9.98\times10^{-4}$.

The binomial test addresses the overall sign imbalance and does not establish
spatial clustering. We therefore treat the north--west versus south--east
arrangement as a spatial pattern visible in the map and do not assign it a
separate clustering significance without a prespecified spatial statistic.


\subsubsection{Exhaustive Eight-Point Reference Combinations}
\label{sec:all-reference-combinations}
\label{sec:rmref-sensitivity}

We recalculated the magnetic-field map for all $\binom{14}{8}=3003$
combinations of eight filtered OFF candidates and compared the maps in an
animation. The resulting reference RMs span 22.2--43.9~rad~m$^{-2}$.
Although field strengths and some individual signs change, several groups
retain their direction throughout this range: positive fields persist in
the western part of the map and around RA $\simeq50$--$51^\circ$,
Dec $\simeq31^\circ$, while a group of negative fields persists in the
southern cloud.

Among the 189 sight lines present in every combination, 77 remain positive
and 35 remain negative throughout; the other 77 change sign. Thus, the
oppositely directed fields in these specific parts of the cloud persist
despite changes in the reference selection. Their persistence supports
the spatial variation in field direction, although the precise boundary
between positive and negative regions remains sensitive to the OFF-point
choice.

\subsection{Field Strength versus Extinction}
\label{sec:av}

Figure~\ref{fig:blos-av} shows $|B_\parallel|$ as a function of visual
extinction. A Spearman rank correlation over the full sample gives
$\rho=-0.524$ with $p<10^{-10}$; restricted to $A_V\leq 3$~mag it gives
$\rho=-0.472$ with $p<10^{-10}$. These p-values use the usual large-sample
approximation and quantify rank association, not an independent magnetic
scaling law.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.48\textwidth]{BLOS_vs_Av.pdf}
\caption{Line-of-sight magnetic field $B_\parallel$ versus visual extinction
$A_V$. Blue points indicate positive fields; red points indicate negative
fields. Error bars show the total uncertainty from the RM measurement,
reference RM, extinction, and chemical model contributions. The vertical green
line marks the reference extinction threshold.}
\label{fig:blos-av}
\end{figure}

Any interpretation of this relation must account for a correlation that is
built into the method. $B_\parallel$ is obtained by dividing
$\mathrm{RM_{cloud}}$ by an electron column that is itself derived from $A_V$
(Section~\ref{sec:ne}), so $|B_\parallel|$ and $A_V$ are not independent
quantities. At fixed $\mathrm{RM_{cloud}}$, the inferred $|B_\parallel|$
necessarily decreases as $A_V$ increases. The observed behaviour should
therefore be read against this methodological dependence rather than against
a null of no correlation.

With that caveat, the data show a negative rank association and large scatter
at all extinctions, where
$|B_\parallel|$ ranges from $\sim 10$ to $\sim 800$~$\mu$G at comparable
$A_V$. This scatter likely reflects a combination of:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item genuine small-scale magnetic structure within the cloud;
    \item geometric projection effects, since the observed $B_\parallel$
    depends on the angle between the total field vector and the line of sight;
    \item variations in ionization fraction not captured by a single mean
    chemical model, which become more severe at high column density where the
    modeled electron abundance is most sensitive to $n_0$ and the cosmic-ray
    ionization rate.
\end{enumerate}

For context, the cloud RM values span $-73$ to $+61$~rad~m$^{-2}$, with the
majority within $\pm 30$~rad~m$^{-2}$ of zero. For most sight lines the cloud
contribution is therefore comparable to or smaller than the foreground and

underlines the importance of accurate reference subtraction and motivates the
sensitivity test in Section~\ref{sec:rmref-sensitivity}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Comparison with Previous Results}
\label{sec:comparison}

Our reference RM of $31.6 \pm 6.1$~rad~m$^{-2}$ is consistent with the
$31 \pm 11$~rad~m$^{-2}$ reported by \citet{tahani2018}, demonstrating
consistency between our VLA-based analysis and earlier NVSS-based work
despite the factor of $\sim 5$ difference in source density.

The two uncertainties are not directly comparable, and we are careful not to
overstate the improvement. Our $\pm 6.1$~rad~m$^{-2}$ is the standard error of
the mean of 8 reference measurements, while our reference sample scatter is
17.2~rad~m$^{-2}$. The uncertainty convention for the $11$~rad~m$^{-2}$
value in \citet{tahani2018} should be checked against that paper before making
a like-for-like precision comparison.

Most importantly, our analysis confirms the spatial pattern of $B_\parallel$
previously inferred from sparse sampling: predominantly positive fields in the
north and west transitioning to negative fields in the south and east. This
reversal was a key input to the 3D magnetic field reconstruction of
\citet{tahani2022b}, and our densely sampled map both supports that
interpretation and reveals additional complexity in the transition region.

\subsection{Are the Inferred Field Strengths Physically Plausible?}
\label{sec:plausibility}

The mean and median $|B_\parallel|$ reported here, 176 and 116~$\mu$G, are
high compared with what is typically inferred at the mean column density of
our sample ($\langle A_V \rangle = 2.43$~mag). Zeeman measurements of the
line-of-sight field in molecular gas at densities of order
$10^3$~cm$^{-3}$ yield values of order $10$~$\mu$G, and the empirical
$B$--$n$ relation of \citet{crutcher2010magnetic} does not reach several
hundred $\mu$G until $n \gtrsim 10^5$~cm$^{-3}$.

The high values should therefore be interpreted as model-dependent
Faraday-weighted field estimates rather than direct measurements of the dense
gas traced by Zeeman splitting. Faraday rotation can weight ionized envelope
material over a long path, while Zeeman observations select specific
transitions and density regimes. The electron-column model and the scalar
reference subtraction are additional sources of systematic uncertainty.

\subsection{Implications for Cloud Magnetic Structure}

The 62/38 split between positive and negative $B_\parallel$ indicates that
the magnetic field in Perseus does not have a uniform orientation along the
line of sight. The spatial segregation of field directions, rather than random
intermixing, suggests that this variation reflects large-scale magnetic
structure rather than small-scale turbulent fluctuations.

\citet{tahani2022b} interpreted the $B_\parallel$ reversal as evidence for an
interaction between two distinct magnetic field systems, possibly associated
with compression by the Per-Tau supershell from one direction and a second,
previously unidentified structure from another. Our densely sampled map is
consistent with this picture. We note, however, that the width of the
transition region between positive and negative fields cannot yet be measured:
with a mean nearest-neighbour separation of $\approx 1.4$~pc, our sampling
places only an upper limit of a few parsecs on the transition width, and a
sharp interface and a gradual one are not distinguishable with the present
data. Distinguishing them would require either a deeper survey with higher
polarized source density or complementary tracers within the transition zone.

The large scatter in $|B_\parallel|$ at high extinction
(Section~\ref{sec:av}) may indicate that the ordered large-scale field is
accompanied by small-scale tangled or turbulent components, or that projection
effects become increasingly important as sight lines traverse longer paths
through the cloud. Disentangling these will require comparison with
plane-of-sky field measurements from dust polarization.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

Several systematic uncertainties affect our $B_\parallel$ measurements.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A single scalar reference RM.} Over a $13.8$~deg$^2$ field,
    the Galactic foreground RM is not necessarily constant, and our reference
    sample itself spans 6.0 to 58.6~rad~m$^{-2}$. A spatially varying
    foreground model would be preferable. We leave comparison with a spatially
    varying Galactic Faraday-sky reconstruction to future work.

    \item \textbf{Chemical model assumptions.} The adopted $n_0$, $T_0$, and
    $G_0$ represent typical conditions but may not capture local variations in
    density, temperature, or UV field strength. Our sensitivity analysis,
    varying these parameters by factors of 2--4, shows that chemical model
    uncertainties contribute $\sim 30$--50\% of the total error budget for
    most sight lines.

    \item \textbf{Electron column density geometry.} The plane-parallel,
    front--back symmetric slab model is an idealization. Real molecular clouds
    have complex 3D structure that affects both the total electron column and
    the weighting of different field components along the line of sight.

    \item \textbf{RM complexity.} Some sight lines may contain multiple
    Faraday-rotating components, for example intervening H~II regions, that
    are not resolved by RM Synthesis. Paper~I flagged potentially
    Faraday-complex sources, but residual contamination cannot be excluded.
\end{enumerate}

A full 3D magnetic field reconstruction combining our $B_\parallel$ map with
plane-of-sky polarization measurements from \textit{Planck} or ground-based
observations is left to future work. Such an analysis would provide stronger
constraints on the field geometry and could test specific predictions of the
interacting-structure formation scenario.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We have presented line-of-sight magnetic field measurements across the Perseus
Molecular Cloud derived from VLA Faraday rotation observations of 197
background polarized sources. Our main conclusions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item The reference RM for the Perseus region is
    $31.6 \pm 6.1$~rad~m$^{-2}$ (standard error), with a reference sample
    scatter of 17.2~rad~m$^{-2}$, consistent with previous estimates based on
    lower-density RM catalogs.

    \item The derived $B_\parallel$ values range from $-991$ to
    $+858$~$\mu$G, with a median absolute strength of 116~$\mu$G. The
    absolute strengths are broad and model-dependent.

    \item The spatial distribution of field directions shows a coherent
    pattern: 122 of 197 sight lines (62\%) have positive (toward-observer)
    fields concentrated in the northern and western cloud regions, while 75
    (38\%) have negative fields in the south and east. A two-sided exact
    binomial test gives $p=9.98\times10^{-4}$. The sign split is sensitive to
    the reference zero point, which is set by the stability analysis. This
    supports the field reversal previously reported at
    lower sampling density.

    \item The width of the transition between the two field regions is not
    resolved by the present sampling; we place an upper limit of a few
    parsecs.

    \item The inferred absolute field strength has a negative rank association
    with $A_V$ ($\rho=-0.524$ overall), but this association is partly induced
    by the dependence of the modeled electron column on $A_V$.

    \item Our map provides the most densely sampled $B_\parallel$
    measurements of the PMC to date and new observational constraints on its
    magnetic field structure and formation history.
\end{enumerate}
